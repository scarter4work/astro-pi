EZHazeKill

Removes the flat sky pedestal and colour cast left after gradient extraction — a process of the EZTools module, version 1.0.0.0. Requires PixInsight 1.9.6 or later.

What it does

After a gradient has been removed (GraXpert, DynamicBackgroundExtraction, GradientCorrection), an image often still carries a flat haze: a constant sky level (the pedestal) that is a little different in each colour channel (the colour cast). EZHazeKill measures the sky on the darkest parts of the image and subtracts a constant from each channel so that

  • the sky becomes neutral grey (the cast is removed), and
  • part of the pedestal above a target level is removed,

without pushing the sky into its own noise and without clipping dark pixels to zero.

It works on linear images and on stretched (non-linear) images, in any sample format (8-, 16- and 32-bit integer, 32- and 64-bit floating point). Grey-scale images are supported: there is no colour cast, so only the pedestal is treated.

Using it

Open the process: Process > BackgroundModelization > EZHazeKill.

  • Real-Time Preview (the open circle at the bottom left of the process window) shows the result on the image while you move the sliders, through the image's own screen stretch, at any zoom and on previews. It shows exactly what Execute writes: the sky is measured once on the full image, not on the reduced preview, and measured again only when the image changes.
  • Apply (the square at the bottom left), or dragging the triangle onto an image, corrects that image. The correction is one step in the image's history and is undone with Ctrl+Z.
  • Measure reports, in the Process Console, the sky level, noise and cast of each channel of the active view and what Execute would subtract with the current settings. The image is not changed.
  • The triangle also makes a process icon with the current Amount, Neutralize and Target. Icons work in a ProcessContainer and in an image's history like any other process.
  • On a preview, EZHazeKill measures and corrects the preview's own pixels.

An image that contains NaN or infinite samples is refused with the message EZHazeKill: the image contains NaN or infinite samples and left unchanged; the Real-Time Preview shows it unchanged.

Parameters

ParameterIdDefaultRangeEffect
Amountamount0.50–1 Fraction of the pedestal above the target that is removed. 0 keeps the sky level; 1 brings the sky down to the target (or to the noise floor, if that is higher).
Neutralizeneutralize1.00–1 Fraction of the sky colour cast that is removed. 1 makes the measured sky neutral grey; 0 leaves the cast.
TargettargetLevel0.050–0.5 The lowest level the sky is brought down to. A sky that is already at or below the target is left at its level: EZHazeKill never raises the background.

These settings are fixed:

SettingValuePurpose
Tile size64 × 64 pixelsThe image is measured in tiles of this size.
Sky tilesdarkest 10%The tiles the sky level and noise are measured on.
Noise floor4σThe sky is never moved below four times its own noise.
Soft toe2σBelow this level, dark pixels roll off smoothly instead of being clipped.

How it works

  1. Sky and noise. The image is divided into 64-pixel tiles (partial tiles at the right and bottom edges are left out; an image smaller than one tile is measured as one tile). For each tile and channel EZHazeKill computes the median and the MAD. The tiles are ranked by luminance (the mean of their channel medians), and the darkest 10% are kept. For each channel c:
    • the sky level skyc is the median of those tiles' medians;
    • the noise σc is 1.4826 × the median of those tiles' MADs.
    Using the darkest tiles, rather than the whole image, keeps a galaxy or a frame-filling nebula out of the sky estimate. An alpha channel is not measured or changed.
  2. What is subtracted. With S the mean of the channel sky levels and σ the largest channel noise: 
    cast_c   = sky_c - S
T_eff    = max(Target, 4 σ)
pedestal = max(0, S - T_eff)
offset_c = Neutralize × cast_c + Amount × pedestal
    The pedestal is never negative, so the background is never raised.
  3. Soft toe. Each pixel becomes u = x - offsetc. Where u is below ε = 2σ, it is replaced by ε · exp(u/ε - 1). The curve is continuous, has the same slope as u at ε and keeps the order of pixel values. Values above 1 are clipped to 1.

The arithmetic is in double precision; the result is written in the image's own sample format (integer images are rounded to the nearest value). The correction is a constant per channel: a gradient is not modelled.

Console report

Execute and Measure print, for each channel, the sky level, noise, cast and the value subtracted, then the overall sky level, the target and the noise floor, and whether the pedestal is reduced or left as it is.

When the sky noise is more than 3% of the sky level, the image looks stretched, and a note says so. Stretched images are supported; the note is for information. On a stretched image the noise is much larger, so the 4σ noise floor is higher and EZHazeKill removes less of the pedestal. When the floor is above the sky, only the colour cast is removed.

What it does not do

  • Gradients. It subtracts one constant per channel. Remove gradients first with GraXpert, DynamicBackgroundExtraction or GradientCorrection.
  • Object colour. It neutralizes the sky only. A colour in the object itself is left as it is. For example, the magenta tint in OIII regions of one-shot-colour images taken through a quad-band filter is object colour, not haze.

Changes from earlier versions

EZHazeKill was first a script (Script > scarter4work > EZ Haze Kill). This process computes the same correction as script version 1.1.0, sample for sample, and adds the Real-Time Preview, process icons that work in a ProcessContainer and in an image's history, and support for previews. When the EZTools package is installed, the updater removes the script's files (src/scripts/scarter4work/EZHazeKill.js, its .xsgn and doc/scripts/EZHazeKill/), so only the process remains.

The script's versions 1.0.x moved every channel's whole-image median to the target level (0.05) and clipped the result at zero. On stretched images the median includes the object, so 1.0.x subtracted more than the sky and clipped a large part of the image to black. On linear images whose sky was below 0.05 it raised the background. Version 1.1.0 replaced that model with the one described above and added Amount and Neutralize.

Network, programs and files

  • Network connections: none. EZHazeKill sends no telemetry and checks for no updates; updates come only through PixInsight's update system.
  • External programs: none.
  • Files: it reads and writes no files. It works on the open image in memory; saving the image is up to you. The only settings it stores are a process icon's parameters, when you create one.
