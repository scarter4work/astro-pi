EZ Haze Kill

Removes the flat sky pedestal and colour cast left after gradient extraction — script version 1.1.0.

What it does

After a gradient has been removed (GraXpert, DynamicBackgroundExtraction, GradientCorrection), an image often still carries a flat haze: a constant sky level (the pedestal) that is a little different in each colour channel (the colour cast). EZ Haze Kill measures the sky on the darkest parts of the image and subtracts a constant from each channel so that

  • the sky becomes neutral grey (the cast is removed), and
  • part of the pedestal above a target level is removed,

without pushing the sky into its own noise and without clipping dark pixels to zero. The correction is one PixelMath step on the target image, so it appears in the history and is undone with Ctrl+Z.

It works on linear images and on stretched (non-linear) images.

Using it

Open the image and run Script > scarter4work > EZ Haze Kill.

  • Target image — the image to correct (any open image).
  • Measure reports, in the Process Console, the sky level, noise and cast of each channel and what Apply would subtract with the current settings. The image is not changed.
  • Apply measures the image and corrects it.
  • The New Instance triangle (bottom left) saves Amount, Neutralize and Target as a process icon. Dropping that icon on an image applies the correction to it with those settings, without opening the dialog.

Grey-scale images are supported: there is no colour cast, so only the pedestal is treated.

Parameters

ParameterDefaultRangeEffect
Amount0.50–1 Fraction of the pedestal above the target that is removed. 0 keeps the sky level; 1 brings the sky down to the target (or to the noise floor, if that is higher).
Neutralize1.00–1 Fraction of the sky colour cast that is removed. 1 makes the measured sky neutral grey; 0 leaves the cast.
Target0.050–0.5 The lowest level the sky is brought down to. A sky that is already at or below the target is left at its level: EZ Haze Kill never raises the background.

These settings are fixed:

SettingValuePurpose
Tile size64 × 64 pixelsThe image is measured in tiles of this size.
Sky tilesdarkest 10%The tiles the sky level and noise are measured on.
Noise floor4σThe sky is never moved below four times its own noise.
Soft toe2σBelow this level, dark pixels roll off smoothly instead of being clipped.

How it works

  1. Sky and noise. The image is divided into 64-pixel tiles (partial tiles at the right and bottom edges are left out). For each tile and channel EZ Haze Kill computes the median and the MAD. The tiles are ranked by luminance (the mean of their channel medians), and the darkest 10% are kept. For each channel c:
    • the sky level skyc is the median of those tiles' medians;
    • the noise σc is 1.4826 × the median of those tiles' MADs.
    Using the darkest tiles, rather than the whole image, keeps a galaxy or a frame-filling nebula out of the sky estimate.
  2. What is subtracted. With S the mean of the channel sky levels and σ the largest channel noise: 
    cast_c   = sky_c - S
T_eff    = max(Target, 4 σ)
pedestal = max(0, S - T_eff)
offset_c = Neutralize × cast_c + Amount × pedestal
    The pedestal is never negative, so the background is never raised.
  3. Soft toe. Each pixel becomes u = x - offsetc. Where u is below ε = 2σ, it is replaced by ε · exp(u/ε - 1). The curve is continuous, has the same slope as u at ε, keeps the order of pixel values, and never reaches zero. Values above 1 are clipped to 1.

The correction is a constant per channel: a gradient is not modelled.

Console report

Measure and Apply print, for each channel, the sky level, noise, cast and the value subtracted, then the overall sky level, the target and the noise floor, and whether the pedestal is reduced or left as it is. Apply also prints the PixelMath expressions it runs.

When the sky noise is more than 3% of the sky level, the image looks stretched, and a note says so. Stretched images are supported; the note is for information. On a stretched image the noise is much larger, so the 4σ noise floor is higher and EZ Haze Kill removes less of the pedestal. When the floor is above the sky, only the colour cast is removed.

What it does not do

  • Gradients. It subtracts one constant per channel. Remove gradients first with GraXpert, DynamicBackgroundExtraction or GradientCorrection.
  • Object colour. It neutralizes the sky only. A colour in the object itself is left as it is. For example, the magenta tint in OIII regions of one-shot-colour images taken through a quad-band filter is object colour, not haze.
  • Previews. The dialog has no image preview. Use Measure to see what Apply will subtract, and Ctrl+Z to undo.

Changes from earlier versions

Versions 1.0.x moved every channel's whole-image median to the target level (0.05) and clipped the result at zero. On stretched images the median includes the object, so 1.0.x subtracted more than the sky and clipped a large part of the image to black. On linear images whose sky was below 0.05 it raised the background. Version 1.1.0 replaces that model with the one described above and adds Amount and Neutralize.

A process icon saved by 1.0.x keeps its Target; Amount and Neutralize take their defaults. Results differ from 1.0.x.

Network, programs and files

  • Network connections: none. EZ Haze Kill sends no telemetry and checks for no updates; updates come only through PixInsight's update system.
  • External programs: none. It uses only PixInsight's own scripting objects and the PixelMath process.
  • Files: it reads and writes no files. It works on the open image in memory; saving the image is up to you. The only settings it stores are a process icon's parameters, when you create one.
